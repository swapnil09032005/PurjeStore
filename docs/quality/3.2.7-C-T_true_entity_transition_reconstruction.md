# PurjeStore — 3.2.7-C-T True-Entity Transition Reconstruction

> Corrected master investigation of true business-entity identity, workflow state history and chronological transition evidence.

## 1. Purpose

This investigation reconciles the zero-transition result produced by 3.2.7-C by testing the structurally linked workflow fields identified by 3.2.7-C-R against more rigorous business-entity identity rules.

The investigation does not assume that the most repetitive identifier is the state-bearing business entity. Foreign or grouping identifiers such as organizational, hub or vendor keys are explicitly distinguished from business entity identifiers.

## 3. Identity Methodology

Candidate identifiers were evaluated using field-name semantics, table semantics, uniqueness, repetition, history-table context and known business-entity naming patterns.

Identity roles were classified as:

- `BUSINESS_ENTITY_ID` — identifier representing the business object whose state may change.
- `BUSINESS_REFERENCE` — business reference capable of identifying the state-bearing object.
- `ROW_RECORD_ID` — identifier of a physical/current table row.
- `HISTORY_RECORD_ID` — identifier of an individual history/event record rather than the business entity.
- `FOREIGN_GROUPING_KEY` — repeated organizational or grouping identifier that does not establish entity continuity.
- `FOREIGN_OR_REPEATING_ID` — identifier whose semantics do not justify treating it as the business entity.

Identity semantics were prioritized before transition frequency. This prevents a highly repetitive foreign key from being selected merely because it produces many apparent state changes.

## 4. Transition Definition

A confirmed chronological transition requires the same business entity to have at least two usable observations with parseable timestamps, different workflow states and a non-zero temporal interval.

State changes occurring at identical timestamps are retained separately and are not automatically interpreted as chronological transitions.

## 5. Chronology Validation

Original observation order produced **0** negative temporal intervals.

After chronological ordering, **13** timestamp ties were observed.

The chronology check is performed before chronological sorting so that the test does not artificially eliminate negative intervals.

## 12. Limitations

This reconstruction can only establish a chronological transition where the raw CSV contains sufficient entity identity, workflow state and temporal evidence. A table containing multiple workflow values across different entities cannot be interpreted as a state history merely because those values occur in the same table.

Repeated foreign/grouping identifiers do not establish continuity of a single business object. This is why identity semantics are evaluated before transition frequency.

Same-timestamp state changes remain inherently ambiguous unless another ordering mechanism is available.
