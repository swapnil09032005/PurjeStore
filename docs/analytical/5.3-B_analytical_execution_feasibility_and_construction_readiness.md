# 5.3-B — Analytical Execution Feasibility and Construction Readiness

## 1. Purpose

Major Milestone 5.3-B evaluates whether the 26 finalized analytical contracts entering execution review are technically and analytically feasible for controlled construction using the validated PurjeStore cleaned sources and previously established relationship and grain evidence.

The purpose of this stage is to establish an evidence-backed **construction-readiness decision boundary** before any analytical datasets are physically constructed.

This stage does **not** construct the 26 analytical datasets.

The outcome is an execution-readiness register describing source availability, measure availability, dimension availability, temporal feasibility, source-grain evidence, relationship requirements, and construction constraints for each finalized analytical contract.

---

## 2. Scope

The authoritative execution population for this stage is the 26 analytical contracts finalized through Major Milestone 5.2-D and activated through Major Milestone 5.3-A.

The execution chain is:

**5.2-C contract gating → 5.2-D contract specification → 5.3-A analytical question activation → 5.3-B execution feasibility and construction readiness**

The four candidates deferred during 5.2-C remain deferred and are not included in the 5.3-B execution population.

The 26-contract population is therefore intentionally narrower than the original 30-candidate population.

## 7. Measure Feasibility

Each finalized analytical contract was evaluated against the actual cleaned source schema for the measure fields specified in the contract.

The assessment distinguishes between:

* fields explicitly requested by the contract;
* fields actually present in the cleaned source;
* fields unavailable from the source.

No measure was invented or substituted merely to make a contract executable.

Measure feasibility therefore remains tied to the actual 5.2-D contract specification and the physical cleaned-source schema.

---

## 8. Dimension Feasibility

Dimension fields specified by each analytical contract were checked against the corresponding cleaned source.

The feasibility assessment verifies the presence of the requested dimensions without assuming that a dimension is analytically meaningful merely because a column exists.

A physically available field is therefore treated as evidence of schema availability, not as automatic evidence of business significance.

---

## 9. Temporal Feasibility

Temporal fields specified by the finalized contracts were checked against the actual cleaned source schemas.

Temporal feasibility was evaluated using the time definition already established during contract specification rather than introducing new temporal interpretations.

No unsupported date field was substituted for a missing temporal field.

Temporal feasibility therefore remains bounded by the contract's documented time definition and the actual source evidence.

## 10. Grain Execution Feasibility

Construction readiness requires preservation of the analytical grain established during the preceding contract stages.

The assessment therefore considers:

* source-grain statement;
* grain key candidate;
* grain validation status;
* whether the proposed grain can be physically represented by the source;
* whether grain interpretation requires semantic review.

Three contracts have an explicitly documented source-record-grain semantic-review condition:

* `daily_category_metrics`
* `daily_platform_metrics`
* `daily_vendor_metrics`

For these three contracts, the authoritative 5.2-D evidence states:

`One row per source record; business grain not independently established.`

Their grain validation is:

`SOURCE_GRAIN_REQUIRES_SEMANTIC_REVIEW`

Their grain specification status remains:

`SOURCE_GRAIN_SPECIFIED`

These three cases are preserved as explicit semantic limitations.

No grain key was invented to eliminate the limitation.

---

## 11. Relationship Execution Feasibility

Where a contract requires relationship evidence, the assessment uses the previously validated relationship register rather than assuming that a join is safe because two columns appear compatible.

The relationship evidence is evaluated in the context of:

* source and target fields;
* validated relationship evidence;
* cardinality;
* fan-out risk;
* grain preservation;
* population limitations.

The authoritative relationship schema uses:

* `source_column`
* `target_column`

The assessment does not introduce non-existent `source_table` or `target_table` fields into the authoritative relationship evidence.

Relationship feasibility is therefore constrained by the relationships already validated in the project.

---

## 12. Cardinality and Fan-Out Considerations

Construction readiness is not equivalent to simple key compatibility.

A relationship can be technically joinable while still creating duplicate analytical rows or changing the intended grain.

The 5.3-B feasibility assessment therefore retains the previously established cardinality and fan-out evidence as construction constraints.

Where a relationship could require aggregation before joining, that requirement must be resolved during controlled construction rather than bypassed through an uncontrolled join.

No uncontrolled multi-table join was performed during 5.3-B.

---

## 13. Pre-Aggregation Boundary

Pre-aggregation is treated as an execution-control mechanism where relationship structure could otherwise multiply rows.

The presence of a relationship does not automatically authorize a direct join.

If a future construction step requires pre-aggregation to preserve the contract grain, that operation must be explicitly specified and validated during the corresponding construction stage.

5.3-B establishes readiness for such controlled construction but does not physically perform it.

---

## 14. Population and Missingness Considerations

Source row counts and available schema evidence were profiled for the 26 contracts.

Population limitations remain tied to the actual cleaned sources and the population definitions already established in the analytical contracts.

Missingness was considered as an execution-feasibility constraint rather than silently treated as evidence that a field is unusable.

The assessment does not claim that missingness has been eliminated.

It also does not convert source availability into a claim of complete analytical population coverage.

---

## 15. Construction-Readiness Boundary

5.3-B establishes an execution-readiness boundary rather than creating final analytical datasets.

The readiness assessment distinguishes between:

* source availability;
* field availability;
* grain feasibility;
* relationship feasibility;
* semantic limitations;
* construction constraints.

Construction readiness therefore means that the contract has sufficient evidence to proceed into a later controlled construction decision where appropriate.

It does not mean:

* the dataset has already been constructed;
* the dataset is a final business KPI dataset;
* the dataset is approved for dashboard publication;
* an ML target has been selected;
* a causal relationship has been established;
* business impact has been demonstrated.
