## 3. Methodology

The validation followed a controlled sequence rather than constructing joins first and investigating their consequences afterward.

### 3.1 Step A — Relationship Evidence Matrix

The previously validated database relationship evidence was matched against the analytical candidate population. This produced a 52-row candidate-level relationship evidence matrix.

- Candidate population: 52
- Authoritative relationship records: 69
- Relationship evidence was not treated as proof of analytical grain.

### 3.2 Step B — Authoritative Relationship Mapping

The authoritative relationship register contains 69 records. 68 relationships have concrete qualified source and target fields.

The remaining relationship is a validated semantic/polymorphic relationship whose target is determined dynamically by an entity-type attribute. It was preserved as authoritative evidence but deliberately excluded from concrete table-to-table analytical mapping.

### 3.3 Step C — Grain Validation

A total of 201 candidate grain records were available from the structural profiling stage.

Candidate grain fields were then compared with actual values in the cleaned data. This distinction is important because a field that appears to be an identifier from schema or naming evidence does not automatically constitute a unique analytical grain.

### 3.4 Step D — Observed Cardinality

Relationship cardinality was evaluated from observed cleaned-data key frequencies rather than inferred solely from foreign-key declarations.

This prevents a declared or candidate relationship from being incorrectly treated as one-to-one, one-to-many or many-to-one without observing the actual populated key distributions.

### 3.5 Step E — Fan-out and Pre-aggregation Screening

The architecture validation distinguishes ordinary relational cardinality from uncontrolled analytical fan-out.

A one-to-many relationship is not automatically considered erroneous. It becomes analytically important when measures from the parent or child side could be duplicated by a downstream join without first controlling the grain.

Where multiple relationships or measure-bearing child structures exist, pre-aggregation is therefore retained as a required architectural control rather than assuming that a direct multi-table join is safe.

## 9. Analytical Anchor Interpretation

The term 'analytical anchor' in this milestone refers to structural suitability for future controlled analytical dataset construction. It does not mean that a final business dataset has been approved.

An anchor can only be considered structurally suitable when its observed grain, relationship participation, cardinality and potential fan-out behavior are sufficiently understood for the intended future analytical question.

The final analytical dataset must therefore be designed against a specific business question and explicit grain rather than being generated by joining the entire source universe.

## 10. Key Methodological Decisions

- Actual cleaned-data observations were used to validate grain and cardinality.
- Schema-level FK-like signals were treated as evidence rather than automatic analytical approval.
- One-to-many relationships were not automatically classified as errors.
- Potential analytical fan-out was explicitly screened.
- Measure-bearing child structures must be pre-aggregated when their grain differs from the target analytical grain.
- The validated polymorphic relationship was preserved separately instead of inventing a concrete target.
- The 52 analytical candidates were not converted into 52 analytical datasets.
- No analytical dataset was constructed during 4.0.2.
- Raw and cleaned source data remained unchanged.
- Future analytical datasets must be justified by validated business questions and controlled grain.

## 11. Limitations and Remaining Analytical Considerations

- Structural suitability does not establish business relevance.
- A candidate grain field may be technically unique without representing the desired business entity.
- Observed cardinality describes the available cleaned-data population and should be revalidated if the source population changes.
- Relationship-level validation does not by itself determine the final analytical questions.
- The polymorphic ops_audit_log relationship requires entity-type-aware handling if it is later used analytically.
- Future analytical datasets must explicitly document their intended grain, join path, aggregation strategy and measure definitions.
- No unsupported revenue, cost, customer, product or operational business-impact claim is made by this milestone.
